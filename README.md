# nicolo-lombardi.com

Nicoló Lombardi's portfolio as a plain static website: every page is a
ready-made HTML file, so it runs on any static host (GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages, Netlify,
or plain FTP hosting) and search engines can read every project.

## Preview on your computer

With Node installed: `npx serve .`, then open http://localhost:3000.
(Double-clicking `index.html` won't work: browsers block JavaScript modules from `file://`.)

## Changing content

Everything lives in **`js/data.js`**. The comment at the top explains each field.
**After any change, run `node tools/build.mjs`** (needs `npm install` once) to regenerate the pages
and sitemap and to bundle the scripts and styles into `dist/` (fewer, smaller files; the pages load
from there, not from `js/` and `style.css`), then commit and push, `dist/` included.

- **Edit a text**: change `title`, `type` or `description` of the project. The first ~155
  characters of `description` become the Google snippet, so lead with what and for whom.
- **Reorder / hide projects**: edit `workOrder` at the bottom of the file.
- **Add a project**:
  1. Put a full-screen cover image in `assets/` (`.webp` or `.jpg`, ~2560 px wide).
  2. For each video run `tools/prepare-video.sh path/to/video.mov myproject-01` (needs `ffmpeg`).
  3. Copy an existing project block in `projects`, give it a new `id` (it becomes the address
     `/work/<id>/`), fill in the fields, and add the `id` to `workOrder`.
  4. `python3 tools/make-og-images.py` (link-preview image), then `node tools/build.mjs`.
- **Case layout** (WPP Production, El Águila, Malik Cross): a project with `stills` shows its
  film on top, the text, a two-column grid of stills (`img(path, width, height)`), then an
  optional "Behind the scenes" strip (`bts.images`, scrolls on its own) and an optional
  closing film (`closingVideo`, plays muted while on screen). Videos: `MAX=1440
  tools/prepare-video.sh <file> <name>` makes the .webm, .mp4, poster and thumbnail.
- **About text / contact**: edit `aboutMarkup()` in `js/views.js`, then rebuild.
- **Menu links**: edit the menu in `template.html`, then rebuild. A new page needs an entry in
  `tools/build.mjs` like About.
- **3D head**: `npm install`, then `node tools/optimize-model.mjs scan.glb assets/about/head.glb`.
  If a new scan doesn't face the camera, change `FACE_TURN` at the top of `js/head.js`.
  The eyes, nose and mouth positions (`FACE` in `js/head.js`) are measured for this scan; a new
  scan needs them re-measured.
- **Archive pieces** (`js/archive-data.js`): export each scan on a transparent background
  (the originals were on black: the cut-out was made with a threshold + flood fill, see the
  commit that added LA BARRA), then save `<id>-front.webp` and `<id>-back.webp` (about 1400 px
  on the long side), plus `<id>-thumb.webp` (560 px, quality ~78: the wall loads all of them), in `assets/archive/<collection>/`.
  `type` is the category shown in the viewer (Napkin, Sugar Sachet, Cup Holder…); a type containing
  "napkin" folds when clicked, anything else squeezes. `ratio` is front width ÷ height. Leave `country` / `location` empty if unknown (shows —).
  Fonts: Caslon Antique (`assets/fonts/`, used unmodified under its licence), Futura with Jost
  as fallback, Helvetica.
- **Head reactions** (`js/head.js`): drag or two-finger scroll rotates (too much = dizzy, eyes roll);
  move over the head = smile; tap an eye = hurt; tap the nose 3 times = it grows; tap the mouth =
  pucker; tap elsewhere = boop; press and hold, then pull = stretch (Mario 64); idle 14 s = sleeps.
- **Landing intro**: the name and the italic line are `NAME` and `LINE` at the top of
  `js/intro.js`, timings in `T`. It plays when someone enters the site or reloads the landing
  page, not when they come back to it from another page; any click, tap or key skips it, and
  it never plays with reduced motion turned on.

Keep every file under 24 MB so it fits all free hosts.

## Tarot Reader (`/archive/tarot/`)

A small first-person game in the Archive: you sit across a table from a fortune teller of whom
you only ever see the eyes and the hands. She asks what keeps you up at night (love, money,
family, work) and reads for that, in short, everyday lines; after the second card she asks a
question that can steer the reading to another topic. There are no instruction buttons: you
touch the deck and the cards; only her questions offer answers. Poke her eyes five times and
it ends badly (her hand, a broken neck, a glitch, and you're back on the portfolio). Other easter eggs: touch a
candle and it goes out; blow out both and she hands you matches (drag one along the side of the
box to strike it, then touch each candle with the flame); the hourglass turns over and the coin
flips. Leaving with the X, she says goodbye and blows the candles out. On the Archive desktop,
clicking the name asks before leaving; if you go, the files burn and the dots say goodbye. You shuffle the deck, cut it,
she turns up the card at the bottom (the **guiding card**, which colours the whole reading), then
you pick five cards for a cross: Past (left), Present (centre), Future (right), The Shadow
(bottom), Conscious Potential (top). She comments on each card, then reads the whole spread and
closes in a few lines. The reading can be reviewed, kept on the device, saved as
an image or printed. Everything is in English; no AI service is used: the texts come from an
authored database and rules.

```
js/tarot/tones.js       ← HOW THE GUIDING CARD CHANGES THE OTHERS: it gives the reading a tone (kind,
                          careful or restless) and each card has a version of its line for each.
js/tarot/life.js        ← WHAT SHE SAYS: each card's line for love, money, family and work, an advice,
                          a warning, and her questions (whose answers can switch the topic).
js/tarot/cards.js       ← THE CARDS: 22 Major Arcana (art, keywords, every interpretation, her reactions).
                          DECK_IDS = the 21 that are shuffled (all but the Fool by default).
js/tarot/lines.js       ← HER LINES between the cards, and the rules that link cards (named pairs, six
                          oppositions) and build the closing story.
js/tarot/engine.js      the rules: Fisher–Yates shuffle (crypto random), cut (deck = B + A), guiding
                          card = bottom card, five picks, commentary, final reading, synthesis. Pure, tested.
js/tarot/experience.js  the ritual, phase by phase (an explicit state machine), input, saving, resuming
js/tarot/stage.js       the three.js room: low-res render + dither/grain pass, candles, props, camera shots
js/tarot/deck.js        the 21 card objects and their choreography (shuffle, cut, guiding card, fan, cross)
js/tarot/hands.js       her low-poly hands and sleeves (two-bone arms, finger poses)
js/tarot/eyes.js        her eyes (canvas-drawn, ten expressions, blinking, gaze)
js/tarot/dialogue.js    typewriter dialogue (font: Heavyweight, assets/fonts/Heavyweight.woff2);  audio.js: synthesised sound (no files)
js/tarot/summary.js     the written reading, local saving, image export, print
js/tarot/textures.js    procedural textures (wood, cloth, flame, silhouette)
js/tarot/outline.js     GENERATED card silhouette (die-cut notch) for the 3D card edges
```

- **New or changed card art**: put the PNGs in one folder (fronts named with their number first,
  `13_Tarot_Death_Frontal.png`, and `BACK.png`), run
  `python3 tools/make-tarot-assets.py that/folder` (needs Pillow and numpy), then
  `node tools/build.mjs`. The fronts are only resized; the back is framed with the fronts' white rim
  and die-cut silhouette (mirrored), so every card looks the same from behind.
- **Another set of 21 cards**: edit `DECK_IDS` at the bottom of `js/tarot/cards.js` (e.g. put the
  Fool back in place of another arcana).
- **Texts**: every card's texts are in `cards.js`; her phase lines and the linking rules in
  `lines.js`. Run `npm test` after editing: it checks that every card is complete, that no two
  cards share a sentence and that every card has a line for every topic.
- **Classic or Yes / No**: the first choice. Yes or No (js/tarot/yesno.js): shuffle, cut, then for each
  question (four offered at a time from 24, "Other questions" shows four more; up to five) you draw one card and it answers yes, no, maybe or
  "not yet" (`VERDICT`; upside down turns the answer), with its everyday line for that topic.
- **Upside-down cards**: every shuffle turns about one card in four upside down (`REVERSE_CHANCE` in
  js/tarot/tones.js). A reversed card says its REVERSED line, plus a line that depends on the guide's
  tone; a reversed guiding card flips the tone of the whole reading; reversed cards stand on the
  opposite side of the links between cards; the ending counts them and changes their sentences.
- **See the Deck**: after the reading, the end menu shows every card of the deck in order as a 3D
  card (js/tarot/gallery.js): drag to spin it, arrows, swipe or the keyboard to go through.
- **Controls**: mouse, touch or keyboard (Enter / Space: continue, arrows: choose a card or move the
  cut, Escape: put a held card back, M: sound). Every physical action also has a button.
- **Saving**: the reading in progress is kept in `localStorage` (`nl-tarot-v1`), so a reload offers to
  continue it; saved readings go to `nl-tarot-saved`. The same ritual always produces the same words.
- **Development**: `/archive/tarot/?seed=123` makes the shuffle reproducible; `?debug` adds
  `window.__tarot` (phase, ritual, `act()`, screen positions) for browser tests.
- **Missing pieces never block it**: a missing image shows a plain card, no sound file is needed,
  and without WebGL the page says so instead of breaking.

GitHub.

### 4. Finish

- Check `https://nicolo-lombardi.com` and `https://www.nicolo-lombardi.com` on desktop and phone.
- Remove the custom domain from the old ChatGPT site so nothing else claims it.

## ASCII Aquarium (`/archive/aquarium/`)

A tank drawn in glyphs, after the "Devault" preset of DEVAULT's ASCII generator (near-black,
white-to-lavender glyphs, Geist Mono, hairlines, square corners). Inspired by Drawquarium.

- **Depth**: blocky rock terraces at three distances (far, mid, near) fade into haze, with coral
  domes and seaweed on the ledges and marine snow under the surface; the cursor slides them
  (parallax). Far fish are smaller, dimmer and swim behind the mid rocks.
- **Fish**: DEVAULT's letter tiles: each cell a colour tile with a darker glyph, thin seams, a few
  bright glyphs sprinkled. The tile grid stays put; each fish is drawn smoothly underneath (a wave
  along the body that grows toward the tail, slow turns through edge-on, a lean into climbs and
  dives, burst-and-glide), so it glides through the grid. Drawings are shaded as a soft relief lit
  from the top left. Plants are written in letters; rocks in plain strokes.
- **Arriving**: clicking the aquarium on the Archive desktop floods the page with glyph water from
  the icon; the tank then decodes out of scrambled characters. Slow machines drop the glow.

- **Draw**: paint in colour on the pad (fish face right, plants grow up); it turns into tiles as
  you draw. Start from a template (the goldfish is after DEVAULT's tile goldfish) or a blank pad. Release: fish splash in; plants go where you click on the sand.
- **Feed**: click the water. Fish chase flakes, eat, grow; hungry fish slow down and dim.
- **Touch**: click a fish = poke; drag a fish, plant or decor = move it; drop on `[ × ]` = remove.
  Hold the cursor still and fish come to look; sweep it fast and they scatter. Plants bend away.
  Click bubbles to pop them, the chest for a burst.
- **Life**: fish that meet build a bond. Friends school; rivals quarrel (`#@!%`); soulmates court
  (`♥`) and lay an egg that hatches a baby: the two drawings blended.
- **Golden scales** (`◊`): fed fish shed them; click to collect. They buy decor, lights (DEVAULT's
  presets: mono, old monitor, amber, ice) and sand.
- Keys: D draw, F feed, M sound, Esc close. The tank is kept on the device (localStorage `nl-aquarium`).

```

js/aquarium/sim.js    the life: swimming, food, bonds, quarrels, eggs, scales, bubbles
js/aquarium/tank.js   the glyph renderer (layers + parallax), input, the draw pad, the decor panel, saving
js/aquarium/tiles.js  the fish: shaded look, body wave / turn / tilt, letter tiles, hit test

js/aquarium/art.js    ← TEMPLATES, DECOR, LIGHTS, TINTS, prices

```

## Point-art (`/archive/point-art/`)

After Pointer Pointer (pointerpointer.com), with paintings instead of photographs: hold the cursor
still and a painting fills the screen, one of its figures pointing right at the cursor, the
fingertip stopping just short of it; move and it's gone. It opens with a mosaic of pointing hands
(`js/point-art/intro.js`): God's hand from the Creation of Adam, small, in the middle of a white page,
then square tiles of other pointing hands appear around it until they fill the screen, then go out;
a click, tap or key skips it. Then a single line in Helvetica Light ("Hold your cursor still" on a
computer, "Tap anywhere" on a phone). The label bottom right, in Montserrat (`assets/fonts/`, SIL
Open Font License) on a Swiss grid, gives title, painter, date, medium, collection and inventory
number, with a link to the museum's page (or to the painting's Wikipedia article when there is no
museum page for it). At the bottom, small and grey, the credit: Pointer Pointer
(pointerpointer.com) by Pluto, @growpluto on Twitter. The X top left goes back to the Archive:
white closes in on the pointing finger of the painting on screen (or the last one seen), the finger
turns white too, and the Archive appears as the white lifts.

- **The paintings**: all kinds (frescoes, altarpieces, portraits, genre scenes, drawings), the most
  famous first (the Creation of Adam, Leonardo, Raphael, Caravaggio, David, Poussin…), then less
  famous ones from The Met and The Cleveland Museum of Art, so that some finger can point at every
  corner of the screen. All public domain; images from Wikimedia Commons, The Met (Open Access)
  and Cleveland (CC0). `tools/point-art/pointers.json` holds each one: where its image comes from, an
  optional crop, the fingertip and the knuckle the finger starts from (fractions of the whole
  image, placed by eye on close-ups), and how famous it is (`links`: the number of Wikipedia
  languages with an article on it, from Wikidata).
- **Matching** (`js/point-art/match.js`): the fingertip goes just short of the cursor (ideally 0.4
  finger lengths, never more than 2.2), its finger in line with it. Each painting is slid within
  what still covers the screen to do that; when the fingertip can't come close, the painting is
  enlarged (cropped), but never past 1.5 screen px per image px (`MAX_UP`), so the paint stays
  sharp. Fame wins ties (`FAME`); the best few are drawn at random, skipping the latest shown.
- **Images**: 2400 px on the long side (WebP), so there is room to enlarge.

To add a painting, add it to `tools/point-art/pointers.json` and run (Python with
`pip install pillow numpy requests`; `work/` is any scratch folder, it is not committed):

```
python3 tools/point-art/export.py work/cache          # images + js/point-art/paintings.js
python3 tools/point-art/fingers.py adam-god            # the opening's tiles (the id shown first)
python3 tools/point-art/icon.py adam-god saint-john-the-baptist adam-god   # desktop icon, shared picture
node tools/build.mjs
```

Wikimedia throttles bulk downloads: export.py waits as long as it is asked to, one file at a time,
and with a cache folder each image is downloaded only once.

To look for more in the museums' open collections (adds `pip install mediapipe`; the hand model is
at storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/hand_landmarker/hand_landmarker/float16/latest/hand_landmarker.task):
`tools/point-art/fetch_meta.py work/meta.json` lists every open-access painting of Cleveland and The Met,
`tools/point-art/scan.py work/meta.json work/hands.json hand_landmarker.task` finds the pointing hands
in them (index straight and out, the other fingers folded); check each by eye before adding it.
